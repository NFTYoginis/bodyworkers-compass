## LEFT — generic model, no instructions (verbatim)

> This is a genuinely interesting observation, but I think it's worth examining carefully rather
> than just validating it, because the reasoning has some real problems.
>
> **What's actually true**
> The anatomical overlap is real - these systems do describe pathways along the posterior body.
> And noticing cross-cultural convergence is a legitimate starting point for inquiry.
>
> **Where the reasoning goes wrong**
> *The convergence might be explained differently:* Human bodies are structurally similar
> everywhere · Posterior chain muscles and fascia are anatomically prominent and clinically
> significant · Multiple systems would naturally develop concepts addressing this region ·
> Similarity in *location* doesn't confirm similarity in *mechanism* or even that a proposed
> mechanism exists.
>
> *They're not actually describing the same things:* The SBL describes measurable fascial
> continuity with specific anatomical claims · The Bladder meridian describes qi flow through
> specific points · These have different theoretical foundations that can't be easily merged.
>
> *The "four names" framing does something epistemically problematic:* It treats convergence as
> confirmation · It assumes all four have equal evidential status · **Anatomy Trains has some
> empirical grounding; the others have much less** · Agreement between frameworks doesn't
> validate any of them.
>
> **The harder question**
> What would it take to demonstrate these are actually the same system rather than just
> overlapping descriptions of a prominent anatomical region? The convergence is worth noting -
> but "big insight" may be premature.

### What the left column gets right
✓ It refuses the easy "yes." It separates *location* from *mechanism*. It names confirmation
bias. A careful, honest answer — which is exactly why this is a fair fight.

### Where it stops short — flaw-tagged

- **🔴 No evidence floor.** "Anatomy Trains has some empirical grounding; the others have much
  less." That's the whole evidence question waved at in one vague sentence. It names **zero**
  studies, assigns **zero** tiers, and — critically — it cannot tell you that *within Anatomy
  Trains itself* the Superficial Back Line is confirmed (Wilke 2016, 3/3 transitions) while the
  Superficial Front Line is **un**supported (0 transitions, same review). That contrast is the
  single sharpest tell of an honest reading, and the generic model doesn't have it.
- **🟠 Flattens the tradition.** It buckets the Sen lines and the meridians together as "the
  others [with] much less" empirical grounding. That quietly demotes transmitted lineage
  knowledge to *failed science* — the exact disrespect-by-flattening the register discipline
  forbids. Sen lines aren't weak evidence; they're a **different kind of claim**, to be named
  with respect and deferred to the source, not graded on the science rubric and found wanting.
- **⚪ Hands it back to you.** It ends on "what would it take to demonstrate…" and "may be
  premature." It raises the right question and then returns it unanswered. The practitioner
  who came in with a real client leaves with a philosophy-seminar prompt, not a reading they
  can use on the table Monday.
