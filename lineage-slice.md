# One lineage slice — raw source → the decision it drove

A single, real proof-of-lineage: the lived source, the build decision it forced, and why
this one slice is committed while the rest of the corpus stays with the tradition. Read the
raw source **and** the choice it produced.

---

## 1 · The raw source

Across fifteen years studying with Pichest Boonthumme in Northern Thailand — beginning in
2005 — I was shown the same thing over and over: low-back discomfort was rarely treated at the site of the pain
itself. The sessions moved through the legs, the hips, the surrounding tissue first — the
attention went to how tension *traveled* through the body, not to where the symptom showed up.
The Sen lines were the map for that exploration. But the emphasis was never on the named line.
It was on watching what actually changed in the person in front of you.

The deeper lesson was almost never about a named line in isolation. Pichest worked from
relationship, from movement, from compensation, from observation. The line was a guide. The
body was the teacher.

> "Pichest taught me to trust what changed in front of my eyes, not what I wished was true."

## 2 · The decision it drove

That training is the reason the Compass has evidence tiers at all — and the reason the Sen
model sits where it sits inside them. The Sen tradition stays included and respected, because
it is a coherent lineage framework that still guides skilled practitioners. But it is not
presented as equivalent to modern fascial research or biomechanical evidence in the places
where direct scientific support is absent. The map is preserved, its limits are stated out
loud, and the user sees both at once. That is the Sen-deferral rule — *name the line, defer the
depth* — and "outside the testable frame" as the Sen tier rather than grading Sen on a science
rubric it was never built to pass.

## 3 · Why this slice, and not the rest

This is the honest middle ground, and it is the cheapest honest thing to expose. Lineage is
real — thousands of practitioners have transmitted and refined these ideas across generations.
But lineage is not the same as a scientific citation, and pretending otherwise would betray
both. Rather than inflate the tradition or dismiss it, the Compass makes the distinction
visible: the user can see what comes from research, what comes from anatomy, what comes from
clinical observation, and what comes from tradition. This slice proves the lineage is *lived*
without dumping the teaching — the depth still belongs to the tradition, to Pichest's source,
and to Gabe's books and decks, which is where that knowledge lives.
